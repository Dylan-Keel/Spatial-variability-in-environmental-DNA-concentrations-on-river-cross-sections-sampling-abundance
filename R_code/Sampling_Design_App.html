How the numbers are calculated

Simple random. CV(n) = CVsite ÷ √n, so n = (CVsite ÷ CVtarget)² (Cochran 1977).

Spatially stratified. Concentration across the width is modelled as a share p of spatially correlated variation (Matérn correlation, ν = 1, with the chosen range) plus a share 1 − p of independent noise. The CV of the mean of n samples at the centres of n equal strips is CVsite × √[(p A(n) + (1 − p)/n) ÷ (1 − p c̄)], where A(n) is the variance of the difference between the sample mean and the true section mean of the correlated part, and c̄ is the mean correlation between two random positions on the section. The tool reports the smallest n at which this CV meets CVtarget.

Simulation. A log-normal field with the chosen CVsite, patchiness, and patch size in a randomly drawn channel up to 3 m deep. It is an illustration; the sample numbers do not depend on the bed shape or on the simulation.

What this tool does not cover
  • It concerns the mean of one cross section on one visit, not day-to-day change and not how well one section represents a longer reach.
  • CVsite from a small pilot is itself uncertain, and the samples needed scale with its square. Round up.
  • Patterns with depth are not modelled. In Keel et al. Chinook salmon eDNA declined with depth, so sample both surface and depth.
  • Detection limits and the volume filtered per sample are outside its scope.
Where the Keel et al. values come from

The green tags give the values measured in one study, Keel et al. (in prep.): six cross sections of the Klamath River, California, sampled during one week in April 2021 with 28 to 35 one-liter samples each, for Ceratonova shasta and Chinook salmon (Oncorhynchus tshawytscha) eDNA. CVsite and width are the range and median across sites and species; patchiness and patch size are one estimate per species from spatial models. They describe those sections on those days, not the Klamath River in general, and are offered as a reference point, not as defaults for other sites.

Keel, D. J., N. A. Som, M. J. Henderson, and A. P. Kinziger. In prep. Spatial variability in environmental DNA concentrations on river cross sections: implications for sampling design and abundance estimation.
