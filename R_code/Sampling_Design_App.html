eDNA River Cross-Section Sampling Design Companion to Keel, D. J., N. A. Som, M. J. Henderson, and A. P. Kinziger. In prep. Spatial variability in environmental DNA concentrations on river cross sections: implications for sampling design and abundance estimation.

How to use this page
  1. Choose a cross section. Simulated: set the site CV, spatial structure, and width yourself, from a pilot study or from the Klamath values given in the hints. Klamath site: load one of the six sections of Keel et al. (2026) and read what the 2021 sampling achieved for either species.
  2. Set the precision you need. The target CV is the standard error of the estimated section mean divided by the mean. The right-hand column gives the number of 1-L samples for that target, and the CV reached with a given n.
  3. Choose placement. Simple random uses the manuscript's formula, n = (CVsite / CVtarget)². Spatially stratified spreads samples evenly across the width and needs fewer samples when concentrations are autocorrelated. For a Klamath site, as sampled uses the positions actually sampled.
  4. Plan in general with the contour maps at the bottom, which give n against site CV and width for any section.
  5. Changing the site CV, spatial structure, width, mean, n, or placement returns the page to a simulated section; the target CV and the contour controls do not.

Cross section

Loads the measured width, mean, site CV, and sample positions of that section, with the species' spatial structure from the fitted models.

Concentration

Mean eDNA concentration on the section. Only scales the colours of the simulated field; a Klamath site uses its measured samples instead.

Coefficient of variation (SD ÷ mean) among single 1-L samples taken across the section, as measured in a pilot study. On the Klamath: 0.40–1.43.

Spatial structure

Share of the variance among samples that is spatially structured, 1 − nugget/sill from a semivariogram. Klamath estimates: 0.60 (C. shasta), 0.33 (O. tshawytscha).

Distance at which the Matérn (ν = 1) correlation falls to about 0.14. Klamath estimates: about 50 m (C. shasta), about 14 m (O. tshawytscha).

Wetted width of the cross-section. Samples spread over a wider section, relative to the correlation range, are less correlated.

Sample placement

Simple random: positions drawn at random across the width. Spatially stratified: one sample at the centre of each of n equal-width strata. As sampled: the verticals and depths used in 2021.

Precision wanted

Precision wanted for the estimated section mean: its standard error ÷ the mean. A target CV is a precision criterion, not statistical power. Rules of thumb: 0.1 research, 0.2 management, 0.3 pilot work.

Precision reached with a given n

Number of 1-L samples collected on the section.

Definitions and formulas
Site CV
Standard deviation of single 1-L sample concentrations on the section divided by their mean. This is what a pilot study measures.
Target CV
Standard error of the estimated section mean divided by the mean.
Simple random placement
CV(n) = CVsite / √n, so n = (CVsite / CVtarget)² (Cochran 1977). This is the manuscript's formula.
Spatially stratified placement
Samples at the centres of n equal strata across the width. Evenly spaced samples converge on the section average, so precision improves faster than 1/√n when the field is autocorrelated. The gain depends on p and on the correlation range relative to the width; it disappears when the range is much larger or much smaller than the section.
As sampled
The same calculation with the positions actually sampled across the width. The precision model is one-dimensional, so paired surface and depth samples on one vertical count as one position.
Intraclass correlation ρ
ρ = p × c̄, where c̄ is the mean Matérn correlation between two random positions on the section. It summarises how alike two samples on the section are and is shown in the sidebar for the current width and range.
What is being estimated
The mean concentration on the sampled cross section on the day it is sampled. Variation shared by every sample on the section is part of that mean, so adding samples keeps improving precision. The page says nothing about how well one section represents a longer reach of river; that takes more sections, not more samples on one.
Simulated field
A log-normal field with the chosen mean and site CV, a share p of its log-variance spatially correlated with the chosen range. The simulated channel is 3 m deep at the thalweg.
Klamath site field
A kriging smoother of the measured concentrations, with the species' p and range, plus a simulated residual drawn with the kriging variance (a conditional simulation, truncated at zero). It follows the samples where they were taken, within the nugget, and carries realistic error between them; re-simulate redraws only the error. Hover shows both the smoothed value and the value with error. Colours run from zero to the highest measured sample. The channel follows the sampled verticals.
Klamath sites
The six cross sections of Keel et al. (2026), sampled in 2021 and numbered from upstream to downstream. Site CV, mean, width, and sample positions are the measured values; p and the range are the species-level estimates from the fitted spatial models (C. shasta: p = 0.60, range about 50 m; O. tshawytscha: p = 0.33, range about 14 m).
