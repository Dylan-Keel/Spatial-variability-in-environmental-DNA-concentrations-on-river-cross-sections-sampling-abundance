How the numbers are calculated

Simple random. CV(n) = site CV ÷ √n, so n = (site CV ÷ target CV)² (Cochran 1977).

Spatially stratified. Concentration across the width is modelled as a share p of spatially correlated variation (Matérn correlation, ν = 1, with the chosen range) plus a share 1 − p of independent noise. The CV of the mean of n samples at the centres of n equal strips is site CV × √[(p A(n) + (1 − p)/n) ÷ (1 − p c̄)], where A(n) is the variance of the difference between the sample mean and the true section mean of the correlated part, and c̄ is the mean correlation between two random positions on the section. The tool reports the smallest n that meets the target.

Simulation. A log-normal field with the chosen site CV, patchiness, and patch size in a stylised channel 3 m deep. It is an illustration; no calculation depends on it.

What this tool does not cover
  • It concerns the mean of one cross section on one visit, not day-to-day change and not how well one section represents a longer reach.
  • The site CV from a small pilot is itself uncertain, and the samples needed scale with its square. Round up.
  • Patterns with depth are not modelled. In the Klamath study Chinook salmon eDNA declined with depth, so sample both surface and depth.
  • Detection limits and the volume filtered per sample are outside its scope.
Where the Klamath values come from

Six cross sections of the Klamath River, California, sampled in April 2021 with 28 to 35 one-liter samples each, for Ceratonova shasta and Chinook salmon (Oncorhynchus tshawytscha) eDNA. Site CV and width are the range and median across sites and species; patchiness and patch size are one estimate per species from spatial models.

Keel, D. J., N. A. Som, M. J. Henderson, and A. P. Kinziger. In prep. Spatial variability in environmental DNA concentrations on river cross sections: implications for sampling design and abundance estimation.
